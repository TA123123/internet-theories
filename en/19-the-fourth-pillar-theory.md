# 💩 The Fourth Pillar Theory (屙大屎理论)

> "The four great wellness theories of the new era: drink big water, sleep big sleep, train lean muscle, take big sh*ts."
> — a viral Douyin post, October 2026

> Chinese: [屙大屎理论](../zh/19-屙大屎理论.md)

## One-liner

The fourth seat at the minimalist-health family table: writing "taking a big one" into your health KPIs — smooth bowel movements are the cheapest physical exam a broke person can get.

## Origin & hype

- Around October 10, 2026, a Douyin post listing "the four great wellness theories of the new era — drink big water, sleep big sleep, train lean muscle, take big sh*ts" went viral, drawing tens of thousands of views.
- Chinese memes love凑 fours. Zhihu discussions noted: "as big water, big sleep and lean muscle, they suddenly carry the aura of having cracked humanity's ultimate health code. But Chinese memes have always liked making fours..." — competition for the fourth seat was fierce, and "taking a big one" won on sheer comedic force.
- Variants circulated simultaneously ("new health four theories" including big water, combat sports, big sleep and big 💩). The "era of grand theories" hashtag kept fermenting on Douyin.
- It's the funniest member of the family — and, awkwardly, the most useful one: gut health is wired directly into water intake (the Big Water Theory), sleep (the Big Sleep Theory), diet and exercise.

## Core claims

1. **Bowel movements are a health barometer**: frequency, form and ease are the body's most daily feedback — more honest than the bathroom scale.
2. **It's the closing loop of the family**: people who hydrate, sleep, eat and move properly rarely need to think about this topic — thinking about it means one of the first four has a gap.
3. **Continues the minimalist-health logic**: no money, no "detox" products — solved with habits.

## The science

- The standard reference is the **Bristol stool scale**: types 1–2 lean constipated, 6–7 lean diarrhea, 3–5 is the ideal zone — it's a crude tool actually used in clinics and research.
- Three ingredients for regularity: **enough dietary fiber** (~25–30 g/day), **enough water** (directly linked to the Big Water Theory), **enough movement** for gut motility.
- Habits: a fixed daily window, don't suppress the urge, don't sit on the toilet for more than ~5 minutes (long sits + phone significantly raise hemorrhoid risk).

## How to practice

- Fiber sources: whole grains, beans, vegetables, fruit, nuts — far cheaper and healthier than "colon-cleanse tea."
- Pair fiber with water: fiber without water can actually worsen constipation.
- Move daily: even a 20-minute brisk walk promotes motility.
- Observe, don't obsess: an occasional anomaly is usually short-term diet/schedule noise — adjust habits before self-diagnosing.

## Caveats

- **It's a meme, not medicine**: "taking a big one" just states the crudest health indicator in the crudest way.
- See a doctor for: blood in stool, persistent abdominal pain, unexplained weight loss, long alternating cycles of constipation and diarrhea — don't self-diagnose these.
- Beware "detox & beauty" / "colon cleansing" marketing: healthy people have no "toxins" to flush, and intestines don't need washing.
- Over-monitoring bowel detail can trigger health anxiety (especially in IBS-prone people) — treat it as a reference signal, not a KPI pressure source.
