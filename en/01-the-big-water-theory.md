# 🌊 The Big Water Theory (大水理论)

> Fewer milk teas and sodas, more big-bottle plain water — healthier and cheaper.
> Chinese: [大水理论](../zh/01-大水理论.md)

## One-liner

Replace milk tea, coffee and sugary drinks with a 1.5–2 liter bottle of plain water. Carry it, finish it.

## Origin & hype

- In October 2026, "drinking big water" topped Douyin trending lists under the hashtag "the whole internet has entered the big-water era" (全网进入大水时代).
- Together with the Lean Muscle Theory and the Big Sleep Theory, it became one third of what media dubbed the young generation's "minimalist health trilogy."
- The standard image of a practitioner: an office desk with a 1.5L jug, filled with boiled-then-cooled water in the morning, empty by evening.

## Core claims

1. **Substitution**: plain water replaces sugary drinks — less sugar, fewer calories.
2. **Cheap**: a big bottle costs one or two yuan, versus twenty-plus for milk tea or coffee.
3. **Control**: psychologists read it as young people reclaiming a sense of control over life through the small, winnable act of drinking water in an uncertain world.

## How to practice

- Carry a large bottle (1.5–2L) and set a small goal: "empty it before clocking out."
- When a soda craving hits, drink water first — make "something sweet" an occasional reward instead of the default.
- Don't chug: the point of the big bottle is having water available all day, not downing it in one go.

## Caveats

- Doctors from top-tier hospitals warn that gulping ≠ scientific hydration; short-term massive water intake carries risks such as hyponatremia.
- Daily water needs vary by body weight, climate and activity — "8 glasses a day" is not universal; urine color is a better signal.
- Some discussions bolt a "strong/weak" gender-performance frame onto drinking water. Don't take it seriously — it's just water.
