# 🌿 The Low-Cost Wellness Theory (低成本养生理论)

> No expensive supplements — value water, sleep, diet and exercise; stay healthy long-term with simple means.
> Chinese: [低成本养生理论](../zh/15-低成本养生理论.md)

## One-liner

The best wellness practices are mostly free: sleep, water, food, walking, sunlight — expensive in persistence, not in price.

## Origin & hype

- Young people's wellness view is undergoing a "quiet but complete reversal": no more chasing expensive health products, no more extreme-body obsession, no more relying on milk tea and coffee to survive — embracing low-cost, sustainable, self-healing and relaxed ways to stay well.
- Part of the same wave as the Big Water Theory and the Big Sleep Theory.

## Core claims

1. **The free stuff works best**: sleep, hydration, balanced diet, moderate exercise and emotional management — all five pillars cost nothing.
2. **Long-termism**: wellness is small daily habits, not a shopping spree.
3. **Anti-"wellness tax"**: don't trust supplements and miracle cures; spend the budget on fresh ingredients and a regular schedule instead.

## Low-cost wellness checklist

- **Water**: carry a bottle; swap milk tea for plain water (bridges to the Big Water Theory).
- **Sleep**: fixed schedule, enough and good quality (bridges to the Big Sleep Theory).
- **Diet**: home cooking over takeout; less sugar and processing, more natural food (bridges to the Anti-Inflammatory Diet Theory).
- **Exercise**: brisk walking, jogging, bodyweight training, stretching — zero barriers (bridges to the Athlete Physique Theory).
- **Emotions**: sunlight, deep breathing, walks, cutting information overload.
- **Checkups**: one basic annual physical — the highest-ROI medical spend there is.

## Caveats

- Wellness is not a substitute for medicine: persistent fatigue, unexplained weight loss or pain — see a doctor first, not a "protocol" first.
- "Low-cost" ≠ "zero-cost": time and patience are the real investment.
- Don't turn wellness into a new source of anxiety and KPIs.
