# 🧾 The Minimalist Consumption Theory (极简消费理论)

> Cut unnecessary consumption; pursue practicality and value-for-money; spend on what truly improves quality of life.
> Chinese: [极简消费理论](../zh/16-极简消费理论.md)

## One-liner

Refuse what you don't need, discard the surplus, detach from desire — take money off the "want" list and spend it on what you "need and use."

## Lineage of the idea

- Origin: Japanese decluttering expert Yamashita Hideko's "Danshari" (断舍离), later merging with Western Minimalism into a global lifestyle movement.
- Evolution in China: the 2026 trend extends from throwing things away to **digital minimalism** (pruning follow lists, unsubscribing pushes, muting notifications) and **relationship minimalism**.
- Within the "×× theory" family it rhymes with low-cost wellness and big water: buy less = spend less = worry less.

## Core claims

1. **Less but better**: one item that beats three, not three you settle for.
2. **Experiences over things**: travel, learning and time together generally out-return most consumer goods.
3. **Practicality and value**: not anti-consumption — every yuan should visibly serve quality of life.
4. **Kill the "latte factor"**: track expenses for a week and be shocked by where the small money goes.

## Practices

- **Three questions before buying**: will I still want it in a week? Do I have space for it? How often will I actually use it?
- **One in, one out**: buy a new garment, retire an old one.
- **48-hour rule**: park non-essentials in the cart for two days before deciding.
- **Subscription audit**: memberships, auto-renewals and app subscriptions, reviewed monthly.
- **Digital audit**: unfollow shopping-account creators, kill nonessential pushes, exit "seeding" groups.

## Caveats

- Minimalism isn't being cheap: education, health and tools deserve real money.
- Don't make "throwing things out" a new KPI, or fall into a throw-out-then-rebuy loop.
- Real minimalism is "enough, just right" — not "less is superior."
