# The Anti-Inflammatory Diet Theory (抗炎饮食理论)

> Cut sugar and ultra-processed food; eat more natural food; keep the body healthy.
> Chinese: [抗炎饮食理论](../zh/09-抗炎饮食理论.md)

## One-liner

Not a specific "anti-inflammation recipe," but an eating pattern that lowers chronic low-grade inflammation by choosing natural, minimally processed foods.

## Scientific background

- Chronic low-grade inflammation is linked to cardiovascular disease, diabetes, arthritis and some cancers.
- Diets heavy in sugar, refined carbs, ultra-processed foods and trans fats raise the body's chronic inflammatory load.
- Foods rich in natural anti-inflammatory compounds (omega-3 fatty acids, polyphenols, antioxidants, fiber) help lower that load.

## Core claims

1. **Eat natural**: vegetables, fruit, whole grains, deep-sea fish, nuts, legumes, olive oil.
2. **Cut the pro-inflammatory**: sugary drinks, desserts, excess refined grains, processed meats, repeatedly deep-fried food.
3. **Patterns over superfoods**: no single food "cures" inflammation — the overall dietary structure matters. A Mediterranean-style plate usually scores high.
4. **The executable version**: make "something sweet" occasional rather than daily; make takeout the exception, not the default.

## How to practice (sample day)

- Breakfast: whole-grain bread + eggs + milk/unsweetened soy milk + a small handful of nuts.
- Lunch: mixed-grain rice + steamed fish/chicken breast + two different-colored vegetables.
- Snack: plain nuts, low-sugar fruit (berries first).
- Dinner: tofu/fish or shellfish + generous vegetables + a moderate portion of tubers.
- Cooking: less braising-in-soy and frying; more steaming, boiling and cold dressings; control sugar and salt.

## Caveats

- Anti-inflammatory eating is a **prevention and wellness approach** — not a substitute for medicine or clinical treatment.
- Don't mythologize "superfoods," and don't turn eating into a gram-precise KPI.
- Extremes (very-low-fat or very-low-carb for years) don't suit everyone; people with chronic disease should consult a doctor/dietitian.
