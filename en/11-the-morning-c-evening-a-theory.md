# The Morning-C Evening-A Theory (早C晚A理论)

> Vitamin C in the morning, vitamin A actives at night — scientific skincare for a youthful state.
> Chinese: [早C晚A理论](../zh/11-早C晚A理论.md)

## One-liner

Vitamin C by day for antioxidant defense, vitamin A (retinol) by night for anti-aging — called the "golden formula" of skincare.

## The science

- **Morning C (vitamin C / ascorbic acid)**: daytime is peak UV and free-radical exposure for skin; vitamin C is an antioxidant that sacrifices itself to neutralize radicals, reducing photodamage and supporting brightness.
- **Evening A (retinol)**: accelerates keratinocyte turnover and collagen production — among the best-evidenced anti-aging ingredients; but it's photolabile (degrades in light) and initially irritating, so it belongs at night with strict sunscreen the next day.
- The synergy: defend by day, repair by night — matching the skin's circadian rhythm.

## Correct usage

1. **Start low**: vitamin C at 5–10%; retinol from ~0.1%, twice a week, building tolerance.
2. **Order**: cleanser → toner → vitamin C (AM) / retinol (PM) → moisturizer → sunscreen (AM).
3. **Sunscreen is a prerequisite**: no sunscreen, no anti-aging — especially while using retinoids.
4. **Storage**: vitamin C oxidizes easily — choose amber bottles, keep away from light, use up promptly.
5. **Don't reverse it carelessly**: sensitive or barrier-damaged skin should repair first; retinoids are contraindicated in pregnancy (breastfeeding: follow medical advice).

## Dermatologists' reminders

- Don't blindly stack high concentrations and many actives — irritation adds up and skin barrier crashes.
- Persistent redness, peeling or stinging means stop and repair, not "push through."
- "AM C PM A" is a popular template, not a universal one: niacinamide, peptides, AHAs all have their place.
